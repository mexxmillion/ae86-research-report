A single photograph can suggest the shape of a car. Turning that suggestion into editable wheels, doors, glass, materials and texture tiles takes a different kind of work: measured reconstruction, controlled experiments, and rejection of changes that only look successful in a log.

Current result: an inspectable research candidate. The original-wheel repair is retained. Material UDIMs have been baked. Glazing, wheel material separation, close-up detail and final surfacing remain unresolved. This is a report of the best approach supported so far, not a finished production asset.

THE TOP-LEVEL VIEW

The best workflow so far

Finish the geometry that defines the asset before committing to its final texture layout. Then let material labels drive UV packing and UDIM assignment. Bake the cleaned surfaces, up-res selectively, and add detail only after checking that the underlying shape, seams and texture projection survive close inspection.

01 / THE STARTING POINT

One photograph, several hypotheses

The supplied AE86 photograph is the visible-appearance reference. It does not specify the back of the car, the interior, hidden wheel surfaces, or the true unlit reflectance of its paint. Generated views and albedo estimates therefore remain hypotheses.

02 / MAKE COMPARISONS FAIR

Normalize before segmentation

Hi3D, Tripo, Meshy and TRELLIS return different scales, orientations and vertex densities. A left wheel in one coordinate frame can land in the roof region of another. The normalization gate must precede geometric clustering, camera projection, part transfer and any cross-model score.

The gate should record the source dimensions, applied transform, canonical axes, ground contact, handedness and resulting bounds. The archived provider gates establish numeric checks; they do not establish correct semantic labels.

The recorded API benchmark balance changed by $5.36 across the provider tests, within the authorized $7.99 cap. Preparing this article and its web previews added no generation API spend.

03 / RECOVER, DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY REPLACE

Use the best source geometry

Learned segmentation proposes useful regions, but connectivity and geometry need to challenge those proposals. A dense source can already contain a wheel, rim or brake surface that an aggressively decimated proxy merges or mislabels. Proxy and source vertex indices cannot be treated as interchangeable.

For this asset, the procedural wheel replacement lost the character of the original: its spokes became skinny rods. A controlled comparison retained one original rear-right Hi3D wheel and reused that same mesh at four wheel pivots. The saved GLB check confirms four nodes sharing one wheel mesh. The independent appearance review preferred the rear-right source for the local rim repair.

That acceptance is local. Soft lugs, wavy highlights and limited tread detail remain. Four shared instances are a structural fact, not a guarantee of perfect arch clearance or ground contact.

04 / CLEAN THE SURFACE BEFORE THE FINAL BAKE

Repair boundaries, then judge the reflection

The body cleanup exposed a practical failure: smoothing disconnected UV-seam vertices opened cracks and distorted the lettering. A weld-before-smooth variant improved those seams in the local comparison. This result supports controlled cleanup, not indiscriminate welding across every intentional opening.

The glass experiment is the clearest rejection

Independently fitted glass polygons did not meet the actual frame. The procedural audit then found one closed opening for each of six panes. The windshield opening has 605 border vertices; the older fitted plane was typically about 19 mm away.

The next experiment attached a seal loft directly to those source-frame vertices and capped its inner boundary. Connectivity and pose-gap checks passed. The rendered cap, however, carried boundary noise into radial ridges and refracted like shattered glass.

“Connected” is not the same validation target as “looks like automotive glass.”

The next supported direction is to let the seal loft absorb the irregular frame edge while constructing a smooth glazing surface inside it. That surface must be validated for smooth normals, seating, thickness, reflections and door motion. It has not yet been demonstrated successfully in this candidate.

05 / MATERIALS DEFINE THE TEXTURE WORK

Separate parts and materials as different labels

A wheel is an articulated part. Rubber, painted metal and a brake disc are material classes inside that part. The texturing workflow needs both. A single “wheel” label is insufficient for sensible texture tiles or an artist’s material masks.

Three radius-only rubber/metal variants were tested on the preserved wheel. Each spilled into the wrong surface and was rejected. The source geometry and UVs are therefore retained while seam-aware material separation remains pending. A correct material mask must survive outer-face, inner-face and tread views.

06 / A STABLE SURFACE, THEN A TEXTURE BUDGET

Material labels → UV packing → UDIMs → up-res

Once borders, normals and material assignments are stable, unwrap and pack within the material groups. Allocate texel density according to close-up use and visible area, and check distortion, overlaps and padding. Separate material tiles are useful for painting; the tile number alone does not prove a production-ready UV layout.

The current V5 bake contains five material tiles: paint at 8192², and glass, rubber, metal and interior at 4096². Base color, roughness and normal maps were produced. Saved-scene checks cover tile bounds, object presence and animated pivots. They do not certify zero overlap or uniform texel density.

Up-res is a transfer and enhancement stage, not a source of ground truth. Preserve coherent lettering, edges and panel seams while increasing texture resolution. Inspect the result against the source image and the previous bake. New scratches, dirt and tiny surface structure must be judged as authored detail, not recovered photographic evidence.

The earlier source-wheel scene combines V5 body UDIMs with original wheel UVs. The newly combined presentation candidate instead preserves the selected pre-bake surface scene and its source UVs; it is not the V5 UDIM delivery. Wheel relabeling and final UV integration remain pending.

07 / ONLY ADD DETAIL THAT SURVIVES INSPECTION

Geometry for silhouette. Shading for microdetail.

Use geometry where the detail changes silhouette, articulation or the shape of a close-up: tire grooves at the shoulder, rim depth, lug nuts, brake discs, glass thickness and opening seals. Use normals, bump and roughness for smaller-scale paint texture, fine scratches and accumulated dirt that do not need to change the outline.

The next surfacing pass should compare a clean baseline with separate additions: geometric tread and hardware, then normal detail, then roughness variation and restrained weathering. Projection paint can transfer visible appearance, but it cannot infer hidden surfaces reliably or repair an incorrect shape.

Pending, not delivered: crisp tire tread, consistent wheel hardware, artist-approved wheel material masks, coherent windshield dirt and a final close-up surfacing pass. Enlarging an image or adding a noise texture is not acceptance.

09 / THE LOOP HAS TO CHANGE ITS MIND

Evidence, rejection, and the next experiment

A useful self-improving harness persists the hypothesis, controlled variables, produced artifacts, numeric checks and independent visual criticism. A rejected result becomes evidence for the next proposal. It must remain possible to inspect the failed branch and understand why it was not promoted.

What the next successful iteration must prove

A smooth, seated glass surface without radial reflections; material masks that respect tire and rim boundaries; stable UV packing after geometry cleanup; coherent lettering and panel edges after baking; and better tread, hardware and imperfections in close-up renders. Each improvement needs a comparison against the retained baseline.

The most useful result of this iteration is a more defensible production order—and clear evidence of where it still fails. The scene is available to inspect; it has not passed final asset acceptance.
